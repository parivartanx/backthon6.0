## Case Study 1: Complicated UTI with ESBL E. coli in Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
* **Authorized Source**: ICMR Treatment Guidelines for Antimicrobial Use in Common Syndromes (2022) & IDSA AMR Guidance
* **Patient Profile**: 68-year-old female, history of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and CKD Stage 3b (baseline eGFR: 28 mL/min, Serum Creatinine: 2.1 mg/dL).
* **Clinical Presentation**: Dysuria, suprapubic tenderness, fever (38.4°C), costovertebral angle discomfort.
* **Microbiology**: Urine culture grew >10^5 CFU/mL *Escherichia coli*. Susceptibility: Resistant to Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Ampicillin, Ceftriaxone, Cefuroxime (confirmed ESBL producer). Sensitive to Nitrofurantoin, Fosfomycin, Amikacin, Meropenem, Ertapenem.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**: Doctor empirically prescribed IV Meropenem 1g TID.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - IV Meropenem is a critical Watch/Reserve tier carbapenem. Using it for stable lower/mild upper UTI drives carbapenem resistance.
  - Aminoglycosides (Amikacin) are strictly contraindicated due to acute-on-chronic nephrotoxicity risk at eGFR 28 mL/min.
  - Nitrofurantoin is avoided when eGFR < 30 mL/min due to inadequate urinary drug concentration and risk of peripheral neuropathy.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Oral Fosfomycin trometamol 3g single sachet dose (for lower tract involvement) OR IV Ertapenem 500mg IV once daily (renally adjusted from 1g OD for eGFR < 30 mL/min) as carbapenem-sparing once-daily outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT).
  - De-escalate from Meropenem, preserving broad-spectrum carbapenems for life-threatening sepsis.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Complete clinical resolution at day 5; repeat urinalysis negative; renal function preserved at baseline eGFR.

## Case Study 2: Pediatric Community-Acquired Pneumonia with Overprescribed Watch Antibiotics
* **Authorized Source**: WHO AWaRe Antibiotic Book (2022) & AIIMS Pediatric Antibiotic Stewardship Policy (2023)
* **Patient Profile**: 3.5-year-old male, weight 15 kg. No known drug allergies.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Cough, tachypnea (respiratory rate 44/min), fever for 3 days, subcostal retractions. Chest auscultation revealed bilateral crepitations.
* **Initial Prescription**: Cefixime syrup 100mg BID + Azithromycin suspension 200mg OD for 7 days.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**:
  - Dual Watch-tier therapy (Oral 3rd gen Cephalosporin + Macrolide) prescribed empirically for non-severe pediatric CAP without atypical features.
  - Azithromycin overprescribing drives high macrolide resistance in *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (>50% in India).
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Per WHO AWaRe and AIIMS guidelines, high-dose oral Amoxicillin (Access tier) is the undisputed first-line choice for pediatric CAP.
  - It covers *Streptococcus pneumoniae* with superior tissue penetration and lowest resistance-driving pressure.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Discontinue Cefixime and Azithromycin immediately.
  - Initiate Amoxicillin dispersible tablets at 40-45 mg/kg/dose BID (total 80-90 mg/kg/day, 600mg BID) for 5 days.
  - Counsel parents on danger signs (stridor, lethargy, grunting).
* **Clinical Outcome**: Fever defervescence within 36 hours; respiratory rate normalized to 24/min by day 3; complete recovery with 0 Watch-tier antibiotics.

## Case Study 3: Septic Shock from Carbapenem-Resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP / NDM-1)
* **Authorized Source**: The Lancet Infectious Diseases (2021) & ICMR Guidance for Treatment of Carbapenem-Resistant Enterobacterales (2022)
* **Patient Profile**: 56-year-old male in Surgical ICU following emergency laparotomy for perforated peritonitis.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Septic shock on day 5 post-op, requiring norepinephrine 0.2 mcg/kg/min, MAP 55 mmHg, WBC 24,000/mcL, Procalcitonin 18 ng/mL.
* **Microbiology**: Blood and peritoneal fluid cultures grew *Klebsiella pneumoniae*. Resistant to all cephalosporins, Piperacillin-Tazobactam, Ciprofloxacin, Meropenem (MIC > 16 mg/L), and Imipenem. Phenotypic and PCR testing confirmed NDM-1 (New Delhi Metallo-beta-lactamase).
* **Stewardship Dilemma**: Meropenem is ineffective. Standard Ceftazidime-Avibactam alone is inactive against metallo-beta-lactamases because avibactam does not inhibit class B carbapenemases.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Deploy synergistic dual beta-lactam therapy: Ceftazidime-Avibactam + Aztreonam.
  - Rationale: Aztreonam is naturally stable against hydrolysis by metallo-beta-lactamases (NDM), but is typically destroyed by co-produced ESBLs or AmpC enzymes. Avibactam protects aztreonam from these co-enzymes, restoring its potent activity.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Ceftazidime-Avibactam 2.5g IV q8h (2-hour infusion) PLUS Aztreonam 2g IV q8h (administered simultaneously).
  - Source control: ultrasound-guided drainage of residual peritoneal collection.
  - Reserve tier Colistin/Polymyxin avoided, preventing severe nephrotoxicity in an already hemodynamically unstable patient.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Weaned off vasopressors within 48 hours; repeat blood cultures sterile at 72 hours; transitioned to floor on day 9.

## Case Study 4: Acute Pyelonephritis in Pregnancy with Ceftriaxone Resistance
* **Authorized Source**: CDC / ACOG Practice Bulletin & IDSA Clinical Guidelines on Antimicrobial Selection in Pregnancy
* **Patient Profile**: 26-year-old primigravida at 24 weeks gestation (2nd trimester).
* **Clinical Presentation**: High fever (39.1°C), rigors, severe right flank pain, nausea, and vomiting. Urinalysis showed pyuria (>50 WBCs/HPF) and bacteriuria.
* **Initial Prescription**: Doctor considered prescribing Ciprofloxacin 500mg IV BID or Co-trimoxazole.
* **Stewardship & Teratogenicity Dilemma**:
  - Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Ofloxacin) are strictly contraindicated in all trimesters of pregnancy due to irreversible fetal arthropathy and cartilage toxicity.
  - Co-trimoxazole is contraindicated (neural tube defects in 1st trimester, kernicterus and neonatal hyperbilirubinemia in 3rd trimester).
* **Microbiology**: Urine culture showed *E. coli* resistant to Ampicillin and Ceftriaxone, sensitive to Cefoperazone-Sulbactam, Cefepime, and Piperacillin-Tazobactam.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Select an FDA Category B antimicrobial safe in pregnancy that overcomes ceftriaxone resistance.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - IV Cefoperazone-Sulbactam 1.5g IV q12h (or IV Cefepime 2g q12h).
  - Hydration with IV crystalloids and obstetric fetal monitoring.
  - Upon 48-hour clinical stability and fever resolution, step-down to oral Amoxicillin-Clavulanate 625mg TID to complete a 10-day total course.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Maternal recovery without complications; normal fetal heart tones; uneventful full-term vaginal delivery at 39 weeks.

## Case Study 5: Surgical Antimicrobial Prophylaxis Prolongation in Elective Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy
* **Authorized Source**: WHO Global Guidelines for the Prevention of Surgical Site Infection (2018) & ICMR AMSP Surgical Policy
* **Patient Profile**: 42-year-old male with symptomatic cholelithiasis undergoing elective, uncomplicated laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
* **Post-Operative Orders**: Resident wrote orders for: "IV Ceftriaxone 1g BID + IV Metronidazole 500mg TID for 5 days post-surgery."
* **Stewardship Dilemma**:
  - Massive misuse of Watch-tier antibiotics for clean-contaminated elective laparoscopic surgery.
  - International consensus and ICMR guidelines strictly specify that for uncomplicated laparoscopic cholecystectomy, NO postoperative antibiotics are indicated.
  - Prolonged post-op prophylaxis increases risk of *Clostridioides difficile* colitis, AMR selection, and hospital costs without reducing surgical site infection (SSI) rates.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Restrict prophylaxis to a single pre-incision dose of Cefazolin 2g IV (Access tier) administered 30-60 minutes prior to surgical incision.
  - Stop all postoperative antibiotic administration.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Discontinue IV Ceftriaxone and IV Metronidazole immediately.
  - Provide adequate oral analgesia (Paracetamol) and wound care.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Patient discharged on post-op day 1; surgical wounds clean and dry at 1-week follow-up; zero surgical site infection.

## Case Study 6: Inappropriate Antibiotic Prescribing for Acute Bronchitis in Outpatient Care
* **Authorized Source**: ICMR Guidelines for Primary Healthcare (2022) & NICE Clinical Guideline [NG120]
* **Patient Profile**: 34-year-old healthy male software engineer, non-smoker.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Productive cough with yellowish sputum for 5 days, low-grade malaise, sore throat. Vitals: SpO2 98% on room air, RR 16/min, Temperature 37.2°C, BP 120/78 mmHg. Chest clear on bilateral auscultation.
* **Initial Prescription**: Patient demanded an antibiotic; physician prescribed Cefuroxime axetil 500mg BID + Azithromycin 500mg OD for 5 days.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**:
  - >90% of acute bronchitis cases in immunocompetent adults are caused by viral pathogens (Influenza, RSV, Rhinovirus, Adenovirus, Coronavirus).
  - Colored (purulent) sputum is due to cellular debris and myeloperoxidase from neutrophils, NOT an indicator of bacterial infection.
  - Inappropriate Watch-tier cephalosporin + macrolide creates community-wide collateral damage.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Diagnostic reassurance: normal vitals and absence of tachypnea/tachycardia rule out pneumonia.
  - De-prescribe both antibiotics.
  - Provide clear patient education on the expected cough trajectory (14-21 days for post-viral bronchial clearance).
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Stop Cefuroxime and Azithromycin.
  - Prescribe supportive therapy: Paracetamol 650mg SOS for fever/malaise, honey-based cough lozenges, steam inhalation, and oral hydration.
  - Provide red-flag safety netting (return if dyspnea, hemoptysis, or high fever >38.5°C develops).
* **Clinical Outcome**: Cough gradually improved by day 12; patient fully recovered with zero antibiotic exposure.

## Case Study 7: MRSA Bacteremia with Vancomycin MIC Creep and Treatment Failure
* **Authorized Source**: IDSA MRSA Treatment Guidelines & CDC Healthcare-Associated Infections Series
* **Patient Profile**: 62-year-old female with central venous catheter receiving chemotherapy for non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
* **Clinical Presentation**: High-grade fever (39.5°C), rigors, chills, erythema and purulent discharge at the central line insertion site.
* **Microbiology**: Blood cultures (2 sets) grew *Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA). Initial Vancomycin treatment started with trough levels at 15-20 mcg/mL.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**:
  - After 96 hours of therapeutic Vancomycin, blood cultures remained persistently positive for MRSA.
  - Repeat E-test showed Vancomycin MIC = 2.0 mcg/mL (Vancomycin MIC creep).
  - High failure rate (>50%) and increased mortality with vancomycin when MRSA MIC >= 2.0 mcg/mL.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Mandatory source control: immediate removal of the infected central venous catheter.
  - Switch from Vancomycin to High-Dose Daptomycin.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Discontinue Vancomycin.
  - Initiate Daptomycin 8-10 mg/kg IV once daily (Watch/Reserve tier bactericidal lipopeptide).
  - Monitor baseline and weekly Creatinine Phosphokinase (CPK) levels for skeletal muscle toxicity.
  - Transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE) to evaluate for infective endocarditis.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Repeat blood cultures negative at 48 hours post-Daptomycin initiation; TEE showed no valvular vegetations; completed 14-day course with full bacteriological cure.

## Case Study 8: Pediatric Acute Watery Diarrhea and Avoidance of Inappropriate Fluoroquinolone FDCs
* **Authorized Source**: WHO AWaRe Pediatric Guidance (2022) & Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) Guidelines
* **Patient Profile**: 2-year-old female, weight 12 kg.
* **Clinical Presentation**: 6 episodes of watery, non-bloody, non-mucoid diarrhea over 18 hours, with 2 episodes of non-bilious vomiting. Child is alert, thirsty, skin pinch goes back quickly (mild dehydration).
* **Initial Prescription**: Private clinic prescribed Norfloxacin + Metronidazole suspension 5ml TID for 5 days.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**:
  - Fixed-Dose Combinations (FDCs) of Fluoroquinolone + Nitroimidazole are irrational, banned under CDSCO recommendations, and harmful in pediatric viral gastroenteritis (Rotavirus/Norovirus).
  - Causes disruption of gut microbiome, dysbiosis, and emergence of quinolone-resistant gut flora.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Acute watery diarrhea without blood (non-dysenteric) is self-limiting and viral. Antibiotics provide zero benefit.
  - First-line medical therapy is rehydration and zinc supplementation.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Immediately stop Norfloxacin-Metronidazole suspension.
  - Prescribe Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) solution: 50-100 mL after each loose stool.
  - Zinc gluconate 20 mg elemental zinc once daily for 14 days (reduces duration and prevents recurrence for 3 months).
  - Continue normal feeding including breast milk and soft diet.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Diarrhea resolved within 48 hours; zero dehydration; full recovery with no antimicrobial exposure.

## Case Study 9: Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP) - De-escalation Protocol
* **Authorized Source**: IDSA/ATS Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia Guidelines & ICMR Intensive Care Protocols
* **Patient Profile**: 71-year-old male admitted 6 days ago for ischemic stroke, now developed fever (38.8°C), purulent sputum, and new right lower lobe infiltrate on chest X-ray.
* **Initial Empirical Therapy**: Started on IV Meropenem 1g TID + IV Vancomycin 1g BID (broad-spectrum Reserve/Watch combination).
* **Microbiology on Day 3**:
  - Endotracheal aspirate culture grew *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (>10^6 CFU/mL).
  - Sensitivities: Sensitive to Ceftazidime, Cefepime, Piperacillin-Tazobactam, Ciprofloxacin.
  - MRSA nasal screen and sputum gram stain negative for gram-positive cocci.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**: Patient is clinically improving (afebrile for 24h, Procalcitonin dropped from 4.2 to 0.8 ng/mL). Doctor intended to continue Meropenem + Vancomycin for full 14-day course.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention (48-Hour Timeout)**:
  - De-escalate immediately: stop Vancomycin (no MRSA).
  - De-escalate from Meropenem to Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h (extended infusion over 3 hours) or Cefepime 2g IV q8h.
  - Shorten treatment duration from 14 days to 7 days per clinical guidelines.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Discontinue Meropenem and Vancomycin.
  - Transition to Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h extended infusion.
  - Complete 7 days total antimicrobial therapy.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Complete radiographic clearance at day 14; hospital discharge to rehabilitation without relapse or *C. difficile* infection.

## Case Study 10: Febrile Neutropenia in Hematologic Malignancy
* **Authorized Source**: IDSA Clinical Practice Guideline for the Use of Antimicrobial Agents in Neutropenic Patients with Cancer & AIIMS Protocols
* **Patient Profile**: 48-year-old female with Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) day +10 post-induction chemotherapy.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) 150 cells/mcL (profound neutropenia). Single oral temperature spike of 38.6°C. Hemodynamically stable, BP 118/74 mmHg, no obvious focus on examination.
* **Initial Prescription**: Resident considered initiating IV Colistin + IV Vancomycin + IV Meropenem simultaneously.
* **Stewardship Dilemma**:
  - Overly aggressive empiric coverage with Reserve antibiotics in hemodynamically stable high-risk neutropenic fever.
  - Vancomycin is NOT routinely indicated in initial empiric therapy of febrile neutropenia unless specific criteria are met (hemodynamic instability, catheter tunnel infection, known MRSA colonization, radiographic pneumonia).
  - Premature Colistin causes severe nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity.
* **Antimicrobial Stewardship Intervention**:
  - Monotherapy with an antipseudomonal beta-lactam is the standard of care for initial empiric management.
* **Verified Resolution & Regimen**:
  - Draw 2 sets of peripheral and central line blood cultures, urine culture, and chest X-ray.
  - Initiate IV Cefepime 2g q8h OR IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g q6h as monotherapy.
  - Re-evaluate at 48-72 hours. Add Vancomycin only if blood cultures grow gram-positive organisms or clinical instability occurs.
* **Clinical Outcome**: Blood cultures negative at 48 hours; neutrophil engraftment (ANC > 500) on day 14; antibiotics successfully discontinued after 48 hours of sustained apyrexia.
